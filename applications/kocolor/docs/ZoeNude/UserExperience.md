# The Zoe Nude Experience

## Discover. Tap. Turn. Reveal.

The experience should feel less like buying something from a vending machine and more like discovering a little beauty store that comes alive just for you.

The customer uses their phone for everything digital. The physical dispenser is deliberately simple, beautiful, and mechanical.

Here is how I envision the complete customer journey.

This is the signature Zoe Nude interaction.

The customer takes hold of the bamboo winding wheel.

They begin to turn.

The wheel drives the mechanical dispensing system. A small generator can harvest some of that energy for the electronics and locking mechanism.

The machine doesn't need a mains connection or conventional battery.

As the wheel turns:

- The mechanism engages.
- The mechanical gears move.
- The product advances.
- A spring-loaded release completes the dispensing cycle.

And then...

A beautiful little music-box chime.

Not an artificial sound from a speaker. A real mechanical sound.

The chime signals that the product has been released.

This should feel playful, surprising and satisfying.

The customer experience is finished, but the Zoe Nude system keeps working.

The app and cloud manage:

| Information          | Purpose                           |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Machine identity     | Location and operational tracking |
| Product sold         | Sales analytics                   |
| Inventory            | Stock management                  |
| Transaction          | Revenue tracking                  |
| Customer preferences | Personalization with consent      |
| Dispensing status    | Operational reliability           |
| Purchase history     | Loyalty and rewards               |

The phone provides the network connection.

The cloud coordinates the business.

The physical machine remains deliberately low-tech.

For inventory accuracy, the first version should distinguish between a purchased product and a mechanically confirmed dispense. We should not silently assume that every authorized purchase resulted in successful delivery.

## What makes this different?

I see three distinct innovations working together:

1\. App-first retail: The customer experience, commerce and intelligence live in software.

2\. Human-powered dispensing: The customer supplies the energy, eliminating the need for conventional powered vending infrastructure.

3\. Experiential retail: The mechanical interaction makes collecting a product memorable rather than transactional.

One final UX principle: The entire experience should take about 30–60 seconds once the customer has chosen a product. Don't make people work for their cosmetics. The crank should be effortless, the music-box interaction delightful, and collecting the product immediate.

That's the experience I would prototype.
