In SeaWeed, these three concepts work together to move you from simple "tracking" to "real-time control." Here is the breakdown:

---

## 1. Categories vs. Envelopes

**Categories** are the labels for your transactions.

- **Categories** (e.g., *Coffee, Lunch, Groceries*) describe **what you bought**.
- **Envelopes** (e.g., *Food & Dining*) are **boundaries** that contain one or more categories.
- **Mental Model**: You might have 5 different categories for food, but they all pull from the same "Dining" envelope.

---

## 2. Budgets vs. Envelopes

This is the most important distinction in SeaWeed's philosophy.

- **Budgets are Retrospective Tracking**:  
  They tell you how much you *wanted* to spend vs. how much you *actually* spent after the fact.  
  They are usually 1-to-1 with a category.

- **Envelopes are Decision-Time Control**:  
  They are enforced **at the moment of purchase**.
  
  - If a budget is exceeded, you see a red bar in a report.  
  - If an envelope is exceeded, the system **intervenes** (via the `InterventionDialog`) before the transaction is even saved.

---

## Summary Table

| Feature   | Concept             | Scope                | Primary Purpose                          |
|----------|--------------------|----------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Category | Descriptive Label  | Single Label         | Organizing and searching transactions    |
| Budget   | Quantitative Target| Single Category      | Tracking performance against goals       |
| Envelope | Behavioral Boundary| Multiple Categories  | Enforcement and real-time intervention   |

---

## The SeaWeed Advantage

While most apps just give you Categories and Budgets (Tracking), SeaWeed uses **Envelopes** to create **"Transparent Friction"**—stopping you at the point of sale so you can make a conscious decision to override or cancel.
