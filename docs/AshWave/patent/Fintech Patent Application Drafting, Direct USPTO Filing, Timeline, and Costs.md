### **Background of the Invention**

**Field of the Invention**  
The present disclosure relates generally to decentralized financial networks, cryptographic consensus mechanisms, and machine learning. More specifically, the present disclosure relates to a blockchain-based financial network that secures decentralized ledgers utilizing a Proof of Useful Work (PoUW) consensus protocol, wherein the computational work performed generates client-outsourced zero-knowledge Succinct Non-Interactive Arguments of Knowledge (zk-SNARKs) to evaluate credit risk through zero-knowledge machine learning (zkML) models.  
**Description of the Related Art** Permissionless blockchain systems rely on robust consensus protocols to provide agreement among mutually distrusting nodes and maintain the integrity of a distributed ledger4. The most prominent consensus protocol historically implemented in decentralized financial networks is Proof of Work (PoW)4. PoW secures the network from Sybil attacks and double-spending by requiring the nodes that participate in the consensus mechanism to solve computationally intensive puzzles, such as finding a particular hash value, solving memory-hard problems, or completing randomized computational tasks4. While highly secure, traditional PoW relies on computationally intensive yet externally useless puzzles, resulting in excessive electrical energy waste and profound systemic inefficiency4. Immense computational power is expended on hash inequalities that possess no intrinsic value outside of securing the blockchain infrastructure4.  
Simultaneously, the global financial industry faces significant challenges in credit scoring and credit risk assessment. Traditional credit scoring models require the exposure of sensitive personally identifiable information (PII) and raw financial data to centralized credit bureaus, creating profound data privacy vulnerabilities and central points of failure for data breaches. While zero-knowledge machine learning (zkML) has emerged as a theoretical solution to enable the creation of credit scoring models that assess borrowers' creditworthiness without exposing sensitive information, the computational overhead required to generate zk-SNARK proofs for complex machine learning models remains prohibitive for individual users or decentralized applications running on standard hardware7.  
To alleviate the energy waste of traditional blockchains, Proof of Useful Work (PoUW) has been explored as an alternative to secure blockchain platforms while also producing real-world value4. However, existing PoUW implementations have largely failed to reach a level of maturity and security suitable for real-world financial deployment because the underlying tasks often lack the necessary cryptographic properties required for consensus, such as asymmetry, unforgeability, progress-freeness, and deterministic verification4. Therefore, a critical need exists for a novel decentralized network architecture that offloads resource-intensive zkML proof computations onto consensus nodes, creating a decentralized marketplace for secure credit scoring that simultaneously provides robust cryptographic security to the underlying financial ledger.

### **Brief Summary of the Invention**

The present disclosure provides a system, method, and decentralized network architecture—referred to herein as the Kinetic Financial Network—configured to solve the aforementioned inefficiencies in both blockchain consensus and privacy-preserving credit scoring. The disclosed system proposes a PoUW consensus protocol that computes client-outsourced SNARK proofs as a byproduct, which are simultaneously used to secure the consensus protocol of a financial ledger4.  
In one embodiment, a decentralized financial network comprises a plurality of consensus nodes communicatively coupled via a peer-to-peer network. Each consensus node is configured to receive a set of unconfirmed financial transactions and a set of credit scoring requests. The credit scoring requests comprise encrypted borrower data and a designated zero-knowledge machine learning (zkML) credit evaluation model7. The nodes engage in a PoUW consensus mechanism wherein the computational puzzle requires the generation of a valid zk-SNARK proof demonstrating that the zkML model was correctly executed over the encrypted borrower data, yielding a cryptographic credit score verification without revealing the underlying sensitive information4.  
The successful generation of the zk-SNARK proof serves as the "useful work" required to mine a block. Upon generating a valid proof, the proving node broadcasts the proof, the resulting credit score outcome, and a proposed block of financial transactions to the broader network. The verification of the zk-SNARK proof by receiving nodes is computationally lightweight, satisfying the strict asymmetry requirement of consensus protocols wherein finding the solution requires significant effort, but verifying the solution is executed rapidly by all mutually distrusting nodes4. Upon widespread network verification, the proposed block is appended to the immutable decentralized ledger, and the proving node is awarded a block reward consisting of digital assets, thereby creating a self-sustaining, decentralized marketplace for zkML computation that provides paramount security to the financial protocol4.  
Furthermore, the system incorporates a dynamic difficulty adjustment mechanism. To ensure consistent block times, the protocol automatically modulates the complexity of the machine learning tasks assigned to the nodes, increasing the depth or parameter count of the required zkML proofs when network hash rate increases, thereby maintaining granularity and parametrization within the consensus environment4.

### **Detailed Description of the Invention**

The present disclosure will now be described more fully hereinafter with reference to the accompanying drawings, in which preferred embodiments of the invention are shown. This invention may, however, be embodied in many different forms and should not be construed as limited to the embodiments set forth herein. Rather, these embodiments are provided so that this disclosure will be thorough and complete, and will fully convey the scope of the invention to those skilled in the art.  
The Kinetic Financial Network operates as a permissionless blockchain system relying on a novel Proof of Useful Work (PoUW) consensus protocol to provide agreement among mutually distrusting nodes4. The system replaces arbitrary hash puzzles with computational tasks that produce valuable results—specifically, the execution of complex machine learning algorithms for credit risk assessment4.  
In a traditional Proof of Work (PoW) system, nodes search for a nonce ![][image1] such that the cryptographic hash of the block header ![][image2]. This process requires trillions of hashing operations per second, consuming vast amounts of electricity to perform operations that are inherently useless outside of the network context. The present system replaces this hash inequality search with the generation of a zero-knowledge Succinct Non-Interactive Argument of Knowledge (zk-SNARK). A zk-SNARK allows a prover to mathematically prove to a verifier that a computational statement is true without revealing any information beyond the validity of the statement itself.  
In the context of the Kinetic Financial Network, a borrower (acting as a client) seeks a credit score evaluation to secure a decentralized loan or interact with a decentralized finance (DeFi) protocol. The borrower possesses private financial data ![][image3] (the witness), which includes sensitive attributes such as annual income, historical repayment data, outstanding debt obligations, and asset valuation. The network utilizes a public machine learning model ![][image4], such as a deep neural network or decision tree ensemble, configured and trained for credit scoring7. The borrower encrypts their data and submits a transaction request to the network mempool. The task outsourced to the consensus nodes is to compute the output ![][image5] and generate a proof ![][image6] that the computation was performed correctly according to the predefined model weights, without ever decrypting or exposing ![][image3] to the public ledger4.  
The consensus nodes (miners) act as cryptographic provers. To mine a new block, a node aggregates a set of unconfirmed financial transactions from the mempool and selects a pending zkML credit scoring task. The node allocates its computational resources (typically utilizing advanced GPU or ASIC hardware optimized for elliptic curve pairing operations) to generate the proof ![][image6]. Because the generation of a zk-SNARK for a complex machine learning model requires massive polynomial commitments, Rank-1 Constraint Systems (R1CS), and arithmetic circuit evaluations, it serves as an extremely difficult computational puzzle that effectively simulates the resource expenditure of traditional PoW.  
To maintain the critical properties required for consensus in an adversarial, permissionless environment, the Kinetic Financial Network strictly enforces several cryptographic axioms, including asymmetry, granularity, unforgeability, and deterministic validation4.  
Asymmetry is achieved because generating the proof ![][image6] requires substantial processing time and memory (often minutes or hours depending on the complexity of the neural network), while the verification algorithm ![][image7] executes in milliseconds, allowing the rest of the network to quickly validate the block4. This ensures that while mining a block is prohibitively difficult to spoof, verifying a valid block adds negligible overhead to the peer-to-peer network propagation.  
Granularity and parametrization are maintained by dynamically adjusting the complexity of the outsourced ML tasks required to mine a block. If total network hash power increases and blocks are being produced too quickly, the network protocol algorithmically increases the required parameter count, layer depth, or batch size of the zkML models required for a valid consensus proof, ensuring the target block time remains stable4.  
Unforgeability is guaranteed by the inherent soundness of the underlying zk-SNARK protocol. A malicious node cannot construct a puzzle task that allows them to precompute the solution, nor can they forge a proof for a false credit score, due to the cryptographic binding of the public inputs ![][image1] and the verification key ![][image8]4.  
Freshness is maintained by cryptographically linking the block header of the proposed block to the cryptographic seed used in the zk-SNARK computation. Specifically, the hash of the previous block must be included as a public input to the zk-SNARK circuit. This prevents replay attacks where a malicious node might attempt to reuse existing solutions or steal recently computed solutions broadcast by other nodes4. Because the proof is mathematically bound to a specific block height and transaction set, it cannot be repurposed.  
Once a consensus node successfully generates the proof ![][image6] indicating the borrower's credit score is valid under the model ![][image4], the node broadcasts the new block containing the financial transactions, the proof ![][image6], and the resulting credit score ![][image9]. The network peers run the non-interactive verification function. Upon achieving consensus (when a majority of the network accepts the proof), the block is finalized. The borrower receives a mathematically verified credit score without exposing their sensitive information, lenders can securely evaluate creditworthiness7, and the mining node receives a block reward of native network tokens, thereby creating a decentralized, self-sustaining marketplace for SNARK computation4.

## **Part II: Statutory Formatting Requirements for the Specification and Drawings**

The preparation of a non-provisional utility patent application requires strict adherence to the statutory guidelines outlined in Title 37 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), specifically sections 1.77 and 1.84. Deviations from these structural requirements can result in Notices to File Corrected Papers, causing delays in the examination timeline and increasing prosecution costs.

### **Arrangement of the Specification (37 CFR 1.77)**

The specification of a utility patent application possesses a required internal structure, which is distinct from the mechanics of electronic filing1. The governing rules for the specification are established in 37 CFR 1.71 through 1.84, with the section-order guidance specifically dictated by 37 CFR 1.77(b) and the Manual of Patent Examining Procedure (MPEP) 608.01(a)1. The regulation utilizes the language "should appear" rather than "must appear," indicating that while it is technically a preferable standard rather than an absolute legal mandate, the USPTO's guidance and nearly universal prosecution practice treat this arrangement as the definitive standard1. Arranging the application in this specific sequence ensures that patent examiners, automated optical character recognition (OCR) systems, and public search databases can navigate the document efficiently1.  
According to 37 CFR 1.77(b), the elements of the application should appear in the following sequential order to streamline examination2:

> 1. Utility application transmittal form.  
> 2. Fee transmittal form.  
> 3. Application data sheet (ADS)2.  
> 4. Specification.  
> 5. Drawings.  
> 6. The inventor's oath or declaration2.

Within the Specification itself, the text sections must be preceded by section headings in uppercase, without underlining or bold type, arranged in the following order2:

> 1. **TITLE OF THE INVENTION**: Placed at the top of the first page (unless provided in an ADS), the title must be brief, technically accurate, and descriptive, preferably two to seven words, and cannot exceed 500 characters8.  
> 2. **CROSS-REFERENCES TO RELATED APPLICATIONS**: Identifies priority claims to earlier-filed provisional or non-provisional applications to secure the earliest possible effective filing date8.  
> 3. **STATEMENT REGARDING FEDERALLY SPONSORED RESEARCH OR DEVELOPMENT**8.  
> 4. **THE NAMES OF THE PARTIES TO A JOINT RESEARCH AGREEMENT**8.  
> 5. **INCORPORATION-BY-REFERENCE OF MATERIAL SUBMITTED ON A READ-ONLY OPTICAL DISC, AS A TEXT FILE OR AN XML FILE**: Required for electronic documents, ASCII plain text files, or Sequence Listing XML files that form part of the permanent record8.  
> 6. **STATEMENT REGARDING PRIOR DISCLOSURES BY THE INVENTOR OR A JOINT INVENTOR**: Used if relying on an America Invents Act (AIA) grace-period exception1. The format specified in 37 CFR 1.77(b)(6) may not be the basis to add information about inventor-originated grace period public disclosures after the filing date without risking a new matter rejection; thus, it must be included at filing10.  
> 7. **BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION**: MPEP 608.01(a) dictates that this section customarily contains two parts: the "Field of the Invention" (stating the art to which the invention pertains) and the "Description of the Related Art" (detailing the problems in the prior art solved by the invention)1.  
> 8. **BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION**: A general statement directed toward the invention rather than the disclosure as a whole, pointing out the advantages and the nature of the inventive concept1.  
> 9. **BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE SEVERAL VIEWS OF THE DRAWING**2.  
> 10. **DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION**: A full, clear, concise, and exact description enabling any person skilled in the art to make and use the invention without undue experimentation, satisfying the enablement and best mode requirements of 35 U.S.C. 1122.  
> 11. **CLAIM OR CLAIMS**: Defines the legal scope of the invention and what aspects are legally protected and enforceable2.  
> 12. **ABSTRACT OF THE DISCLOSURE**: A single-paragraph narrative (preferably under 150 words) allowing examiners and the public to quickly determine the gist of the technical disclosure1.  
> 13. **SEQUENCE LISTING** (if applicable)2.

Furthermore, the physical formatting of the specification requires lines that are 1.5 or double-spaced, utilizing a nonscript font (e.g., Arial, Times Roman, or Courier) preferably in a size of 12, but no smaller than a size of 6, presented in a single column of text3.

### **Standards for Drawings (37 CFR 1.84)**

The visual representation of the invention is governed by 37 CFR 1.84, which establishes comprehensive technical and visual requirements ensuring that patent drawings are clear, consistent, and capable of accurate reproduction12. The USPTO mandates that drawings provide sufficient detail and multiple views to fully disclose the invention's appearance, features, and characteristics12.  
Compliance with 37 CFR 1.84 requires meticulous attention to drafting techniques. The USPTO evaluates line quality stringently; line weights must be sufficiently heavy and dark to remain visible after undergoing digital reproduction and reduction, yet not so thick that they obscure fine details or merge with adjacent lines14. Standard conventions regarding shading, cross-hatching, and graphical representations must be adhered to explicitly12. The regulations also mandate strict adherence to specific sheet margins and view numbering protocols14. Failure to comply with the line weight, margin, or numbering standards articulated in 37 CFR 1.84 will result in the USPTO objecting to the drawings, requiring the applicant to submit replacement drawings prior to the issuance of the patent, thereby adding unnecessary expense and delay to the prosecution cycle12.

## **Part III: Electronic Filing Procedure via USPTO Patent Center**

The USPTO has fully transitioned its electronic filing infrastructure, decommissioning the legacy Public and Private PAIR (Patent Application Information Retrieval) systems and EFS-Web in favor of Patent Center11. Patent Center serves as the unified, modernized tool for filing patent applications online, submitting follow-on documents, and managing prosecution history11. Filing electronically is economically imperative; submitting an original non-provisional utility application in paper format incurs an unnecessary non-electronic filing penalty of $400 for a large entity (reduced to $200 for small entities), which Patent Center entirely eliminates16.

### **Step-by-Step Filing Process**

The following process outlines the strategic workflow for filing the Kinetic Financial Network application directly through Patent Center:  
**Step 1: Authentication and Access** The applicant or registered patent practitioner must log into the USPTO Patent Center utilizing a registered USPTO.gov account authenticated via a two-step verification mechanism19. Once authenticated, the user accesses the secure dashboard to initiate a new submission.  
**Step 2: Selection of Application Type**  
Within the Patent Center interface, the user navigates to the "Submit an application" portal. For the Kinetic Financial Network, the user must select "Nonprovisional Application" and specify the type as a "Utility Patent Application."  
**Step 3: Uploading the Application Data Sheet (ADS)** The process begins with the submission of a web-based or fillable PDF Application Data Sheet (ADS) in compliance with 37 CFR 1.76(c)2. The ADS facilitates the automated extraction of bibliographic data, including the inventor names, applicant information, correspondence address, entity status claims, and domestic or international priority claims21. Patent Center parses the ADS to auto-populate the underlying database, eliminating manual data entry errors that commonly triggered pre-examination notices in the legacy EFS-Web system.  
**Step 4: Uploading the Specification, Claims, and Abstract (DOCX Format)** The USPTO heavily incentivizes the use of the DOCX format for text-based submissions to improve its internal data routing and XML conversion processes. While PDF uploads remain technically permitted, filing a specification, claims, and abstract in a non-DOCX format incurs a punitive surcharge of $43024. Therefore, the Microsoft Word (.docx) file containing the specification for the Kinetic Financial Network—formatted according to 37 CFR 1.77—must be uploaded. Patent Center executes real-time validation on the DOCX file, analyzing font sizes, margins, and section headings. The system provides immediate feedback, allowing the applicant to review rendering discrepancies. It is permissible to submit an application with a system warning, but fatal structural errors must be corrected prior to final submission26.  
**Step 5: Uploading Drawings and Declarations (PDF Format)** Unlike the textual specification, the patent drawings prepared in accordance with 37 CFR 1.84 must be uploaded as PDF files. Similarly, the signed Inventor's Oath or Declaration under 37 CFR 1.63, the executed Power of Attorney (if applicable), and any initial Information Disclosure Statements (IDS) are uploaded as standard PDF documents2.  
**Step 6: Fee Calculation and Transmittal** Once all documents are uploaded and validated, Patent Center routes the user to the fee calculation module. The system assesses the basic filing fee, search fee, and examination fee17. It simultaneously calculates excess claim fees (if the application exceeds 3 independent claims or 20 total claims) and application size fees (for each additional 50 sheets exceeding 100 sheets) based on the entity status declared in the ADS18. For the Kinetic Financial Network claims drafted above (comprising 3 independent and 7 dependent claims, totaling 10 claims), no excess claim fees will be triggered.  
**Step 7: Payment Execution** Payment for all required fees can be executed via a pre-funded USPTO deposit account, electronic funds transfer (EFT), or a major credit card (MasterCard, VISA, American Express, Discover)21. When paying by credit card, applicants must utilize the integrated Patent Center payment gateway. An explicit advisory dictates that applicants must *not* attach the PTO-2038 credit card form as a PDF document within the file upload section, as doing so may expose sensitive credit card information to the public via the Patent Center document repository21.  
**Step 8: Submission and Confirmation** Upon successful payment, the user executes the final submission. Patent Center immediately generates an electronic receipt featuring the assigned Application Number, Confirmation Number, and a time-stamped acknowledgment of all uploaded files. This receipt serves as the official evidence of the application filing date under 35 U.S.C. 111(a)22.

## **Part IV: Strategic Cost Analysis and Entity Classification**

Official USPTO fees are determined by an applicant's entity status, a legal classification under 37 CFR §§ 1.27 and 1.29 that drastically impacts the overall patent budget16. Proper documentation and strategic timing regarding entity status represent the single most impactful cost reduction strategy available to patent applicants16. The Unleashing American Innovators Act of 2022 codified substantial fee reductions, altering the economic landscape of patent prosecution. Under this framework, small entities pay 60% less and micro entities pay 80% less on most USPTO fees compared to the standard large entity rates28.

### **Entity Classification Qualifications**

**Large Entity:** The default status applicable to large corporations and entities that do not meet the stringent criteria for discounts16. **Small Entity (37 CFR § 1.27):** Qualification demands fewer than 500 employees (including affiliates) and no assignment, license, or obligation to transfer rights to a large entity16. This classification saves inventors thousands of dollars over the lifecycle of the patent16. **Micro Entity (37 CFR § 1.29):** Requires stricter criteria, including a gross income limit (e.g., $212,352 annually in 2023), the filing of fewer than four previous non-provisional patent applications, and adherence to the same assignment restrictions as small entities16. Alternatively, university employees can qualify for micro entity status regardless of income if their primary employment constitutes their main income source16. The additional savings over small entity status makes micro qualification highly worthwhile for eligible startups developing technologies like the Kinetic Financial Network16.

### **Additional Patent Filing Surcharges (Strategic Avoidance)**

Patent prosecution fee accumulation occurs rapidly if applications are not drafted strategically. The Kinetic Financial Network application must be structured carefully to avoid punitive surcharges that can quickly drain a legal budget16.

* **Excess Claims:** The per-claim surcharge is steeply enforced to discourage overly broad or repetitive claiming. Each independent claim in excess of three costs $600 for a large entity ($240 small / $120 micro)16. Total claims exceeding twenty trigger a $200 penalty per additional claim for large entities ($80 small / $40 micro)16. Furthermore, utilizing multiple dependent claims—a practice common in European patent drafting but disfavored in the US—incurs a severe $925 penalty for large entities24. By maintaining a claim set of exactly 3 independent and 7 dependent claims, the Kinetic Financial Network application avoids these fees entirely.  
* **Application Size:** If the specification and drawings exceed 100 pages, an extra sheet fee of $450 ($180 small / $90 micro) is applied for each additional 50 sheets18.  
* **Format and Linguistic Penalties:** Filing a paper application incurs a $400 penalty, while filing electronically but failing to use the preferred DOCX format incurs a $430 non-DOCX surcharge16. Additionally, non-English translation charges add $150 per document16.

### **International Expansion Costs (PCT Filing)**

Given the global nature of decentralized financial networks, international protection via the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) is often necessary. A PCT filing runs on a separate track and incurs distinct fees. When the USPTO acts as the International Searching Authority, the search fee is $2,400 for a large entity ($960 small / $480 micro)17. Additionally, an international filing fee is paid to WIPO's International Bureau, covering publication. This fee is $1,667 for the first 30 pages, plus $19 per additional page17. Unlike USPTO fees, this WIPO fee is not reduced by U.S. small/micro entity status; instead, PCT-wide fee reductions depend on the applicant's nationality or residence, with a 90% reduction applying to applicants from certain lower-income countries17. A standard PCT filing alone typically runs roughly $3,000–$3,500 before factoring in attorney time17.

### **Issuance and Long-Term Maintenance Fees**

Upon allowance of the application, the applicant must pay an Issue Fee to formalize the grant. Filing and prosecution costs end once a utility patent is granted, but the cost of keeping it does not17. Under 35 U.S.C. § 41(b), utility patents require maintenance fees payable at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 years to prevent the patent from lapsing into the public domain17. These renewal fees saw a 7–8% hike in 202524.

### **Total Estimated Cost of the Kinetic Financial Network Patent**

The financial assessment of "how much a patent costs" must delineate between official USPTO fees and external attorney fees28. USPTO official fees remain fixed, but attorney fees, which account for the vast majority of the bill, scale aggressively with technological complexity28.  
The Kinetic Financial Network encompasses software, artificial intelligence (zkML), and deep-tech cryptography (zk-SNARKs and blockchain consensus). Because claims in these crowded and abstract technical areas are exceptionally difficult to draft in compliance with the patentability requirements of 35 U.S.C. 101, and because examiners frequently push back against software algorithms as abstract ideas, this invention sits at the absolute high end of the cost spectrum17.  
Drafting an original application of this complexity requires a thorough prior-art analysis and sophisticated claim strategy, commanding attorney fees ranging from $10,000 to $16,000 for the drafting and filing stage alone17. Responding to Office Actions typically adds $2,000 to $5,000 per response in attorney time17.  
Consequently, a realistic estimation of the total cost to prosecute the Kinetic Financial Network application from first draft to grant (excluding long-term maintenance fees and international PCT filings) ranges from **$20,000 to $30,000+** when utilizing standard legal representation17. If the full 20-year lifetime maintenance fees are included, a large entity should budget up to roughly $17,800 in USPTO fees alone over the patent's life29.

## **Part V: Timeline and Prosecution Lifecycle**

The patent prosecution timeline requires strategic management to minimize the aforementioned costs, particularly extension fees and RCE surcharges16. The lifecycle of a software patent like the Kinetic Financial Network involves several distinct phases.  
**Filing to First Office Action:** Applications filed early in the fiscal year often receive faster initial examination16. Generally, applicants wait between 14 to 18 months from the filing date to receive a First Action on the Merits (FAOM) from the examiner. During this period, at the 18-month mark, the application will typically be published by the USPTO. If an applicant desires a significantly faster timeline, they may utilize Track One Prioritized Examination. This program guarantees a final disposition within 12 months, but requires a steep upfront fee of $4,515 for large entities, which includes the processing fee (with proportional reductions for small/micro entities)24. Notably, an alternative "rocket docket" program for expedited design patent examination was officially suspended and removed in April 2025, but this does not impact utility applications24.  
**Information Disclosure Statements (IDS):** Throughout the examination period, applicants hold a duty of candor to submit known prior art references to the USPTO via an Information Disclosure Statement (IDS). The 2025 rules instituted new tiered, volume-based fees for IDS submissions to prevent "reference dumping." Citing more than 50 documents applies a $200 fee, exceeding 100 documents costs $500, and over 200 references incurs an $800 cumulative charge24. Therefore, applicants must strategically focus their IDS submissions on highly relevant references to avoid these penalties24.  
**Response Windows and Extensions:** When an examiner issues an Office Action (typically an initial rejection based on prior art), the USPTO provides a shortened statutory period of three months to respond27. Applicants must maintain strict docketing hygiene to reply within this window. Failure to do so triggers the escalating monthly extension penalties detailed in Table 2, capping out at a five-month extension before the application is considered abandoned16.  
**Conclusion of Examination:** The average timeline from the initial filing to the final issuance of the patent spans 24 to 36 months, assuming one RCE and two rounds of prosecution. If the examiner determines the invention is novel, useful, and non-obvious30, a Notice of Allowance is issued. Following the payment of the Issue Fee, the patent is officially granted. Because the USPTO now heavily penalizes late continuations with surcharges up to $4,000, the applicant should analyze the market and file any strategic continuation applications or divisional applications early in the process—ideally while the parent application is still pending and well before the 6-year threshold24.  
By restricting claim counts below the penalty thresholds, utilizing Patent Center correctly with DOCX files, executing rapid responses to avoid extension fees, and claiming proper entity status, applicants can effectively navigate the escalating USPTO fee schedule and secure robust intellectual property protection for next-generation decentralized financial technologies.
